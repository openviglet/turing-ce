Migrate to Turing

Switching from Algolia or Elasticsearch?

Keep faceted, typo-tolerant search — and add RAG, cited chat and agents — without shipping your content to someone else's cloud. Viglet Turing ES is open source and self-hosted: your documents and embeddings stay in your own infrastructure.

The import path

Three moves, not a rewrite

  • 1

    Describe the schema as code

    Turn your Algolia index settings or Elasticsearch mappings into a versioned field manifest and POST it. Turing converges the live schema and flags breaking changes before they run. No schema handy? Derive a draft from sample documents.

  • 2

    Bring your records over

    Bulk-index from your source of truth (or a records/_source export) through the REST index API, or point a connector at AEM, WordPress, a database or files. Your data flows straight into your own Solr, Elasticsearch or embedded Lucene index.

  • 3

    Swap the front-end client

    Replace InstantSearch or the elasticsearch-js client with the Turing React SDK or the zero-dependency vanilla-JS SDK — headless hooks and components for search, facets, autocomplete and cited chat, so the same UI now runs on your infrastructure.

Your data stays in your infrastructure

A hosted SaaS keeps your index — and increasingly your users' queries — in someone else's cloud. Turing runs entirely on your own hardware: your documents, embeddings and query logs never leave your network. Apache 2.0, no per-record pricing, no lock-in.

  • Self-hosted under Apache 2.0
  • Content & embeddings stay on-prem
  • Bring your own LLM & search engine
  • No per-record or per-search billing
